When a founder books a call, they usually arrive with a diagnosis already in hand. "Our close rate is the problem." "We need more leads." "Sales just isn't converting." They're rarely wrong about the *symptom*. They're almost always wrong about the *phase*.

A revenue engine has six phases, and they run in order: who you sell to, where demand comes from, how deals move, whether your operations tell the truth, how the team runs, and how much leverage you've built. Trouble in a later phase is usually a tax being paid on an earlier one nobody's looked at in two years.

## "Our close rate is the problem" (P3)

The founder points at conversion. We score the engine. Phase 3 is genuinely low — but so is Phase 1. They sell to anyone who'll buy, and "why you over the alternative?" gets a different answer from every rep.

You can't fix a close rate downstream of a positioning problem. The deals are stalling because they were never qualified against a real ideal-customer profile in the first place. Fix P1 and a chunk of the P3 leak closes on its own.

## "We need more leads" (P2)

Sometimes true. Often it's a Phase 4 problem wearing a Phase 2 costume. The CRM is a graveyard, so nobody actually knows which leads convert, so the instinct is "more." More of an unmeasured thing is just a bigger unmeasured thing.

When the CRM becomes trustworthy and inspected, "we need more leads" frequently turns into "we need to stop dropping the ones we have."

## "The team isn't executing" (P5)

The cadence is the tell. No operating rhythm, or a weekly meeting that's drifted into status theater where nothing gets decided. The team isn't failing to execute — there's no system that turns information into decisions on a schedule. That's not a people problem. It's an un-built cadence.

## Why the order matters

Fixing a late phase while an early one leaks is how founders end up "working on revenue" for a year with nothing compounding. The diagnostic exists to find the *earliest* weak phase, because that's the one whose fix pays for the phases above it.
